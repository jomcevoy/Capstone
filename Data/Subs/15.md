#### Frame the BBO task as a classification problem and reflects on how logistic regression, SVMs or neural nets could capture the boundary, including trade-offs in misclassification vs exploration
Framing Black-Box Optimisation (BBO) as a classification task shifts the goal from predicting exact objective values to partitioning the search space into discrete "High-Performance" and "Low-Performance" zones. To do this logistic regression fits a linear decision boundary (a hyperplane) that separates high-performing points from low-performing ones based on a chosen threshold. Support Vector Machines (SVMs) find the optimal hyperplane that maximises the margin between the two performance classes. Neural networks use layers of non-linear activation functions to approximate highly intricate, winding boundaries across the search space.

When using a classifier for BBO, tuning the model's tolerance for error directly dictates whether the overall optimisation strategy prioritises exploration or exploitation. In SVMs, the regularisation parameter C  controls the penalty for misclassification. Adjusting this parameter alters how the model interacts with unknown territory. Low C (Exploration Prioritised): The model maximises the margin width and allows points to be misclassified or fall inside the margin buffer. High C (Exploitation Prioritised):The model penalises misclassifications heavily, forcing the decision boundary to perfectly wrap around every single data point to separate classes
#### Justifythe choice of model (linear regression, SVM, neural network) for guiding the search, with reasoning around interpretability vs flexibility
To guide the Black-Box Optimisation (BBO) in terms of  boundary classification, Support Vector Machines (SVMs) would be selected as the best choice of model alongside Gaussian Process. This choice is justified by a strategic balance between interpretability and flexibility, The other  two models have serious weaknesses that restrict their usefulness. Linear Regression has unmatched interpretability but  completely lacks the flexibility required to guide a global search. Conversely neural networks possess the infinite flexibility needed to map any complex, winding boundary, but they lack interpretability and require vast amounts of data to generalise
