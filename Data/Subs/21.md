#### Identify at least one key assumption about the functions or optimisation process, and explain how this assumption might shape or limit results.
By using a standard scikit-learn Matern kernel, we assume that the function’s spatial correlation structure, smoothness (v=1.5), and length-scales (l) are uniform across the entire input search space. 

Because a standard stationary kernel cannot have a short length-scale in one corner of the space and a long length-scale everywhere else, the Gaussian Process is forced to compromise. It will either over-smooth the cliff edges, treating the sharp drop-off as a gradual slope and under-smooth the rest of the space. To prevent the kernel from failing we have implemented bounded regions for search - thus limiting exploration and potentially results. 

#### Identify gaps or potential biases in the data set by considering query distribution, unexplored regions or sampling patterns across the search space.
Given the limited initial dataset and the restricted number of queries available for exploration the resulting search space now resembles a vast desert with a high yielding cluster in an isolated oasis. The acquisition functions suffer from tunnel vision and are heavily drawn to these safer pockets.

A further bias is self inflicted particularly in the larger dimensions, where, based on the correlation analysis we have restricted certain dimensions and created specific search boundaries. Leaving a large portion of the search space unexplored. 

Finally the curse of dimensionality and the sparsity bias. In a high-dimensional hypercube, 33 to 53 data points provide practically zero spatial coverage. Because the data is so sparse, the distance between any two sampled points is too large for the model to accurately compute.

#### Identify one significant limitation of the approach, considering computational constraints, sampling biases, assumptions about function behaviour or other factors affecting validity or generalisability.
The most significant limitation affecting the validity and generalisability of this approach is the vulnerability to premature convergence caused by tunnel vision on local exploitation, driven in part by the initial data set and in part by the low number of query opportunities.

The initial data set contains a high percentage of poor- performing points with only small pockets of stronger results. Even with 5 to 6 rounds of exploration - for the higher dimensional functions the search space is just as sparse. The exploitation may find a reasonable answer in the lower dimensional functions, but it is unlikely to find the true global maxima in the higher dimensional functions.
There is also limited generalisation as the optimisation path is heavily dictated by the initial .npy data files distributions. If a different researcher used the same modelling with an altered set of starting points the models could easily converge on an entirely different region.
