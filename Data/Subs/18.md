### 18.1: Refining your strategies for the black-box optimisation challenge

#### Which hyperparameters did you choose to tune, and why did you prioritise them?
Halfway through the project there has been a mixed set of results; for three of the functions query 6 brought further increase in outputs, two have flattened performance and three have yet to better either the base or query 1 performance. For these last three a tuning of the hyperparameters was required. 

In Function 2 this meant increasing the length scale from 0.3 to 0.5 and shifting the acquisition function from EI to UCB, The increased length scale widens the search function and while EI is great for refining a known peak, the problem explicitly warns that we might get stuck in a local optimum. For this function the alpha (noise) variable was also increased to help the model separate peaks from noise.
 
#### How has hyperparameter tuning changed your query strategy compared to earlier rounds?
The hyperparameter tuning in the earlier rounds encouraged the acquisition models to explore more rather than just build on the existing predictions. Choosing UCB with a higher kappa, higher alpha variable and higher length scales stopped the model fixating prematurely on known peaks.

As we move into the second phase of the project hyperparameter tuning becomes less broad and is driven by findings in the predictions. Results that lie on the boundary, proximity radius, or dimensions that have little impact on performance can be adjusted for through hyperparameters. Allowing the model to focus creating queries to point to the true drivers of performance. 

#### Which tuning method(s) did you apply (manual adjustment, grid search, random search, Bayesian optimisation, Hyperband), and what trade-offs did you notice?
Tuning was performed manually supported by statistical metrics. These were important for refining the search space, scaling the input dimensions, and creating individual length scales. Grid Search and Random search were way out the reach given the limited query budget (13 queries) provided. Similarly building separate BO or Hyperband models would be inefficient and would not have enough data to train. Manual adjustments is the simplest method but is also a riskier method and would be less effective if the other options were viable.
  
#### As your data set grows to 16 points, what limitations of your model become clearer through tuning (e.g. overfitting, irrelevant features, diminishing returns)?
The datasets in the lower dimensional functions should start to see a slow down in performance as the search space becomes clearer. With 16 data points in a 2D space, the Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition function will begin to suffer from extreme diminishing returns.

Similarly in the higher dimensions where some of the dimensions are not important the model will waste capacity trying to map a complex, jagged landscape for an irrelevant feature that is actually just pure, uninformative noise.
 
#### How might you apply hyperparameter tuning techniques to larger data sets in future rounds of the BBO capstone project submissions or more complex models in future ML/AI projects?
Scaling hyperparameter tuning to larger datasets in future rounds or handle complex deep learning architectures, would require a  transition from local, manual heuristics to automated, scalable, and multi-fidelity tuning frameworks. This could mean combining the Bayesian optimisation methods with a Hyperband to tune Large Language Models (LLMs) and Deep Neural Networks during their training phases.
