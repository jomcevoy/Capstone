### 16.1: Refining your strategies for the black-box optimisation challenge

#### How did the ideas of hierarchical feature learning influence the way you thought about structuring or refining your optimisation strategy this round?
Hierarchical feature learning is the concept that involves breaking down complex data into simpler, more manageable parts, which can be particularly useful in neural networks like Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). 

In the context of structuring and refining, my optimisation strategy, hierarchical feature exploration occurs in stages:

Examination layers: Using mutual_info_regression as an initial layer to filter out irrelevant or noisy input features..

Exploration layers: Feeding only the high-impact features into the surrogate models (scikit-learn GPs or Random Forests).

Exploitation layers: Refining the kernel parameters, adapting  the acquisition models to hone in on the optimal outputs through pore exploitation.

In this round I continued to verify the understanding of the irrelevant or noisy input features, and focused the high impact features into surrogate models that encouraged exploration but allowed for some exploitation.  

#### You saw how breakthroughs such as AlexNet and ImageNet classification reshaped expectations in AI. What parallels do you see between those leaps in performance and the incremental improvements you make in your capstone submissions?
 
The leap from traditional hand-crafted features to AlexNet proved that raw data paired with the right model architecture scales exponentially better than manual guessing.

At the moment the incremental improvements from the capstone submissions rely heavily on manual adjustments and heuristic intuition, yielding small, unpredictable gains. The use of SciPy acquisition maximisers helps improve the performance through a series of random searches and selecting the absolute best optimum found among the K runs as the next black-box evaluation point, and I will continue to investigate other optimisers that help automate more of the process.

#### When training neural networks, people often weigh trade-offs between depth, complexity and training efficiency. Did you encounter similar trade-offs in deciding whether to explore widely or exploit known promising regions in your queries?
 
This trade-off directly mimics the balance between exploration and exploitation governed by the acquisition functions

Complexity & Depth (Exploration): Seeking areas of high uncertainty requires evaluating widely dispersed inputs. This adds complexity and carries a high opportunity cost, as a failed query yields no immediate leader board progress.

Training Efficiency (Exploitation): Querying areas near known local minima offers immediate, reliable rewards. However, over-exploiting can lead to premature  convergence, trapping the model in local maxima.
 
#### Reflecting on the building blocks of neural networks (inputs, activations, loss, gradients, weight updates), which of these concepts helped you think differently about how your model learns from the data you’ve accumulated so far?
Loss Gradients in neural networks reflect the performance of a model and can be backpropagated through the model to help adjust the weights of the network to minimise the loss function. The difference between the expected improvement from a new query in the black box project and any actual improvement is a similar indicator of performance and I will be tracking these differences more closely going forward as it will help indicate if the current strategy is working

Weight Updates - The addition of the extra_input and extra_output vectors into our notebooks serves as a structural "weight update." Each new data point shifts the landscape of the Gaussian Process, refining the surrogate's accuracy just as backpropagation adjusts weights to reduce loss. Viewing the build up of data  as a continuous training process rather than a static log.
