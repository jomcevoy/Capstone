Identify key trade-offs faced during the BBO capstone project, explain how exploration vs exploitation (or short vs long-term strategy) were balanced and provide reasoning.
 
Optimising eight distinct black-box functions under a strict 13-query limit forced deep strategic compromises. The execution revealed three fundamental trade-offs:
Information Gain vs. Immediate Score: Early weeks sacrificed immediate performance to map out the response surface. This information paid off in late-stage runs, but risked returning highly volatile, low-scoring queries in the short term.
Model Complexity vs. Sample Efficiency: The transition from a smooth RBF kernel to a jagged Matern kernel with ARD length scaling allowed the surrogate model to better reflect complex landscapes.  However, adding these hyperparameters increased the risk of model mismatch when data points were sparse.
Dimensional Freedom vs. Search Space Convergence: Freezing or bounding dimensions based on early statistical insights (such as binding Function 7's primary feature to zero) effectively collapsing the search space but doubling the output against baseline. The trade-off was a permanent blind spot: if the early statistical review was distorted by noise, the true global maximum could be completely locked out.
The project balanced the exploration-exploitation strategy by adjusting the variance parameters to either favouring exploration or ultimately ignoring it.
Phase 1: High-Variance Exploration (Weeks 1–3) : Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) with a high exploration hyperparameter paired with a smooth RBF kernel. With limited baseline data, the acquisition function forced the optimiser to sample aggressively away from the baseline, ensuring more of the structure was mapped before the search space narrowed.
Phase 2: Prior-Informed Exploration (Weeks 4–8) : Switching to a Matern kernel, tuning the noise alpha, and introducing Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) length scale differentials. Pure random exploration was highly inefficient as dimensions increased. By shrinking the length scales on high-importance dimensions, the model was forced to explore only along the axes that actively drove the objective function.
Phase 3: Risk-Averse Exploitation (Weeks 9–13) : Collapsing the acquisition functions to low-kappa UCB and low-xi Expected Improvement (EI), combined with hard coordinate bounding boxes on critical dimensions. Bounding the search space based on the preceding 8 weeks of data stopped the optimiser from taking large exploratory leaps, focusing entirely on exploiting localised maxima.
 
Articulates a significant lesson learned from the project, explains why it matters, connects it to future competitions or real-world ML projects and includes a reflection on surprising aspects of the process, results or other participants’ strategies.
 
The most significant lesson learned from this project is that smoothness is a dangerous assumption in black-box optimization. Relying on mathematically pristine landscapes and assuming that these real-world response surfaces behave like continuous, gently rolling hills. The breakthroughs came in Weeks 4–8 when switching to a Matern kernel with tuned noise alphas, which explicitly acknowledged that these functions were jagged and noisy.
 
In real world machine learning applications these assumptions could prove costly.
Therefore it is important to prioritise Kernel Architecture Over Acquisition Functions: If your underlying surrogate model doesn't accurately mirror the physical roughness of the space, changing your acquisition strategy is completely pointless.
Similarly building Roughness and noise into function: Future pipelines should test for landscape ruggedness using initial spot-checks rather than defaulting to generic parameters.
 
There were a couple of surprising results of the process
Firstly the needle in the haystack - for the radiation function (1) and the dynamic function (4) - the hits were lucky - but could only have happened with a definite exploration strategy.
Secondly, the impact of dimensionality reduction. Collapsing the search space using basic statistical analysis completely outperformed letting a complex model try to navigate a high-dimensional space on its own.
All in all the capstone project has helped re-inforce strong principles around the importance of understanding the problem (analyse, visualise); simplifying the problem (dimensional reduction); account for distractions (kernal adoption); measuring and managing returns (documenting progress). Of these the last is the area I need to improve upon the most.
