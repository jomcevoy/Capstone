For Functions 7 and 8, the second queries resulted in small score reductions par for the course during exploration. #### How do you balance exploration against exploitation? Do you focus more on areas known to perform well, or are you still sampling from untested regions?
In most cases the focus is still on exploration given the limited number of samples for the acquisition function to fit to. Where the sample size is higher relative to the dimensions the models provide a more balanced mechanism of regional exploitation-focused exploration.

When optimising the surrogate model with a ‘reduced’ dimensional space (areas known to perform well), setting the bounds on the length-scale hyperparameter is critical. Small sample sizes require proper bounds to prevent the model from over fitting (length-scales too short, creating sharp spikes at data points) or under fitting (length-scales too long, smoothing out the true surface).
 
#### How would SVMs change your approach? Could you use a soft-margin SVM to classify high vs low performance regions? Would a kernel SVM help if the response surface is non-linear?
Using a Support Vector Machine (SVM) as part of the approach particularly for functions 4 (highly complex) , 7, and 8 (high sparsity) could help reduce the area under consideration for exploitation. Moving away from trying to perfectly surrogate highly complex or unstable landscapes, and shifts you towards spatial partitioning.

Soft margin SVM permits some data points to be misclassified or fall inside the margin buffer. By tolerating these minor errors prevents over fitting, and creates a smoother, more generalised boundary. Soft-margin SVM introduces a tuning parameter (C) that allows focus to shift from exploration to exploitation. A kernel SVM is highly effective and necessary for non-linear surfaces, which these response surfaces are.

#### What limitations of your current model become apparent as data grows? Is it over fitting? Do any features or dimensions emerge as irrelevant?
As data grows in higher dimensions, the standard UCB framework struggles. The volume of the search space grows exponentially, making the current sample sizes too small to guide the model effectively.  The model does risk over fitting if  the length-scale hyperparameter are reduced too much. Function 4 (dynamic/noisy) and function 1 (radiation spikes) are in danger of over fitting.

Functions 3, 5, and 7 revealed less relevant features that can be factored out. The limitation here is that the model did not automatically ignore them; it required manual intervention to set the focus of the analysis strictly onto the important feature sets. These will need to be factored back in once the analysis moves into the final exploitation phase.
