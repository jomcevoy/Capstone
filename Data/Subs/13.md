The lower dimensionality of the 2D functions (Functions 1 and 2) allows for a visualisation of the search space. The observed outputs showed high variance regions where queries reduced uncertainty but did not improve the overall score. In response to this model behaviour, the strategy changes from general exploration to an intensified exploration phase by increasing the acquisition function's exploration factor (k). Exploration is explicitly prioritised over exploitation because the local maxima are not yet distinct enough to confidently target. The primary trade-off of increasing exploration now is the temporary delay in optimising the score; however, it ensures that after 3 or 4 more queries, the high-variance zones will be sufficiently minimised to allow for a highly accurate local exploitation phase later.

The observed outputs from the initial query reaffirmed that Function 3 possesses a single feature of high importance, with the objective score increasing along this single dimension. In response to this model behaviour, the strategy shifts from a broad search to a targeted, one-dimensional exploration. Exploration along this specific axis is prioritised to investigate the potential for alternative local maxima before committing to local tuning. The trade-off here is the risk of wasting budget on sub-optimal points along an otherwise promising dimension, but it prevents the acquisition function from prematurely locking into a local optimum based on a single successful data point.

Function 4 (the dynamic function) returned a surprise positive output on the first query, contrasting sharply with previous negative baselines. Because this function exhibits dynamic behaviour, the model's current variance estimates are unreliable. Simple experiment with the second query is to repeat the first to gain some insight into the dynamic nature of the function.

This prioritises understanding model/system behaviour over immediate optimisation. The clear rationale is to establish whether the positive output was a transient anomaly or a repeatable trend. The major trade-off is the zero-progress cost to optimisation; we sacrifice an iteration step to gain vital baseline data, which prevents the acquisition function from chasing a "ghost" peak if the function's landscape has already shifted.

The initial query for Functions 5 and 6 yielded significant score uplifts, validating the initial exploratory approach. Model behaviour indicates that two dominant features drive these functions. The strategy now shifts from global exploration to a regional exploitation-focused exploration, specifically targeting the boundaries ("corners") adjacent to the new high-value outputs. This prioritises exploitation by leveraging known successful regions to map out where the optimisation trend is heading. The trade-off is the potential introduction of confirmation bias into the surrogate model; by clustering queries near the successful boundaries, we risk completely missing a superior global optimum located in the unmapped, lower-priority regions of the search space.

For Functions 7 and 8, the initial queries resulted in minimal score uplifts, indicating that the current search regions are likely stuck in flat plateaus or poor local minima. In response to this stagnant model behaviour, the strategy shifts heavily away from exploitation toward aggressive global exploration. This is structurally executed by increasing the acquisition function's exploration factor (k) from 2.0 to 5.0 within the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) framework.

Broad exploration is heavily prioritised to force the model to ignore predicted mean rewards and instead hunt within completely unvisited, high-variance regions of the parameter space. The primary trade-off of this drastic (k) increase is a complete sacrifice of short-term optimisation progress; the algorithm will deliberately sample high-risk areas that may yield even lower scores. However, this trade-off is necessary to discover the true topography of the functions.
