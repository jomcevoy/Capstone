### 20.1: Refining your strategies for the black-box optimisation challenge

#### How do scaling laws influence your current query choices? Do you see diminishing returns or steady improvements?
Scaling laws inform the query strategy by defining the mathematical relationship between your sample size and the dimensionality of the search space. In Bayesian optimisation, the search space volume grows exponentially with dimensionality, a phenomenon known as the curse of dimensionality.

When the number of dimensions is high and the sample size is low, blind exploration yields diminishing returns. As dimensions increase, the surrogate model cannot accurately calculate the lengthscale or covariance between points because the distance between samples becomes uniformly large. This produced random returns for the first 3-4 queries in the larger dimensional functions.

Steady improvements occur when the sample density is high enough for the model to construct an accurate predictive landscape, allowing the acquisition function to pinpoint global maxima. Even with good sample density though the acquisition function will generally produce diminishing returns as the optimisation approaches the peak. Steady improvements  also occur where dimensionality can be factored out.  By reducing the dimensions the sample density per feature increases, allowing the Gaussian Process or surrogate model to provide more reliable, balanced guidance. 

A good example is Function 7 which has 6 dimensions but only three have a significant influence, and has shown steady improvement once these were adjusted for.

#### What trade-offs between cost, robustness and performance are shaping your strategy now?
 
In this Black Box exercise the proxy for cost is the limited number of queries available and that has been fixed at thirteen. Since this is fixed there are no trade offs to be made but given the limited amount the strategy has been  heavily influenced by it.

The strategy needed to balance the resources dedicated to improving the underlying accuracy and robustness of the surrogate model against the need to deliver a maximum return with the limited query budget. The period of exploration was limited to the first 5-6 queries allowing the model to map a large portion of the search space. Hyperparameters such trusted regions, lengthscales and specific kernels, have been introduced to help improve the robustness of the models and to help identify areas of real opportunity. Over the final 3-4 weeks the focus will shift exclusively to exploitation to focus all resources on performance.



#### How do you balance predictable optimisation with the risk of sudden but uneven emergent capabilities?
The balance of predictable optimisation with the risk of sudden but uneven emergent capabilities is driven by the performance of the individual surrogate function. Where the function predicts and delivers predictable gains then the model is left to continue to optimise based on its existing configuration. Prioritising robustness over chasing potential emergent opportunities.
However, as with Functions 3, 4 and 5 above the unexpected drops in performance forces an adaptation to the models - which includes factoring in the chance for exploration of emergent opportunities. Knowing that the cost constraint of the black box limits that opportunity in the long run.
