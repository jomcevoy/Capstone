### 17.1: Refining your strategies for the black-box optimisation challenge

#### Explain how the idea of progressive feature extraction shaped refinements to the BBO strategy
The concept of CNN progressive feature extraction fundamentally shifts a Black-Box Optimisation (BBO) strategy from a blind search to a structured, hierarchical learning process.Instead of treating the objective function as a single incomprehensible puzzle, the BBO strategy learns the optimisation landscape in layers of increasing abstraction.

CNN lower feature extraction : Early layers detect pixel gradients, edges, and orientations. ~~ BBO Exploration: Initial sparse sampling captures local function gradients, noise levels, and directional trends, mapping the fundamental "edges" of the landscape without needing a global picture.

CNN middle feature extraction: Middle layers combine edges and gradients to identify textures, geometric shapes, and distinct motifs. ~~ BBO Surrogate Modelling: The algorithm aggregates local data points to construct a surrogate model, mapping broader landscape shapes such as basins of attraction, ridges, and valleys.

CNN complex feature extraction: Deep layers synthesise shapes and motifs to recognise complex, holistic objects (e.g., a face or a car). ~~ BBO Exploitation: This strategy focus on the  details surrounding the best estimate maxima and pinpoints the global peak..md

CNN Classification layer: The final fully connected layer outputs a class probability, collapsing complex high-dimensional features into a concrete decision. ~~ BBO Convergence: The algorithm triggers termination criteria (e.g., maximum evaluations reached or minimal step size), collapsing the search from probability distributions down to the final, optimal parameter set.
 
#### Draw a parallel between CNN breakthroughs (e.g. LeNet) and incremental improvements in the BBO capstone project
Before LeNet, computer vision relied on humans manually designing feature extractors. LeNet automated this by forcing the network to learn its own features directly from raw pixels. This is similar to the progression from manual heuristic search and global exploration when you have limited data points to using automated exploitation acquisition functions to focus in on the true maxima in the capstone functions,

AlexNet scaled LeNet by leveraging GPUs, deeper layers, and Dropout to handle massive, complex datasets without over-fitting, in the same way the technical strategies start with simple robust scientific computing libraries (like Scikit-Learn) and develop into more complex deep learning frameworks (PyTorch and TensorFlow)

Finally ResNet introduced skip connections to solve the vanishing gradient problem, allowing information to flow across ultra-deep networks without degrading. The inclusion of adaptive re-starts or historical memory archives mirrors skip connections
 
#### Describe a trade-off in the query strategy in relation to CNN training trade-offs (depth, cost, overfitting)
Increasing network depth in CNNs (adding more convolutional layers) improves feature representation but drastically escalates the computational cost  and training time).There is a similar trader of in BBO optimisation. If your query strategy is too "deep" or complex, the convergence time to the maxima may take too long, looking for the perfect answer. The trade off in between query strategy sophistication against a strict computational budget (number of queries allowed).

A massive CNN trained too aggressively on limited data will overfit. It memorises the high-frequency noise and specific "textures" of the training set rather than learning generalised global features. In BBO, focusing the query strategy purely on localised, high-value regions (aggressive exploitation) is the exact equivalent of overfitting.
 
#### Identify at least one CNN building block (convolution, pooling, activation, loss) and explains how it informed model optimisation
In CNNs max-pooling extracts the maximum value in a local patch (e.g., a 2×2 neighbourhood). This discards precise spatial tracking in favour of structural presence, i.e. it cares if a feature (like an edge or a corner) exists, not its exact pixel location.

In high-dimensional black-box landscapes, modelling every point with equal precision is computationally impossible (limited query budget). Pooling informs the use of Local Aggregation or Trust Regions in BBO. Instead of forcing your surrogate model (e.g., a Gaussian Process) to track every micro-fluctuation across the entire search space, partitioning the space and extract only the "peak" or average performance from regional sample clusters

Edge computing has certain advantages; the first of which is the reduction in latency. The speed of what happens at the edge can almost be instantaneous. So, you can really treat your data very quickly. Also edge deployment means not needing to be connected to the cloud, providing critical connectivity.
